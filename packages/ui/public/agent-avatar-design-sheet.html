Agent Avatar Design Sheet

Every agent gets a robot head, drawn from a hash of its name and its owner's user id. Nothing is stored: the same name always draws the same robot, and a rename draws a new one. This sheet shows every part the generator can pick, the colors it picks from, and the code name for each, so a visual change can be named exactly.

Each label under a part is the literal value in AvatarTraits. Unless a section says otherwise, every tile uses the same head and colors and changes only the part being shown. The percentages are how often each option appears across 20,000 generated avatars.

Anatomy

An avatar is built from up to eight part categories, stacked in a fixed order. A gap of AVATAR_GAP (3.2 units) is cut between parts that touch, which is what makes them read as separate pieces.

  • headThe base outline. 9 shapes, filled with colors.head.
  • topWhat sits above the head: hat, bolt, crown, siren, bug eyes, or a cap drawn inside it.
  • faceHow the robot looks out: eyes, a visor, shades, or wink marks.
  • derpThe eye arrangement when face is "eyes": 11 layouts.
  • sidesEars or antennae on both sides of the head.
  • bandingHorizontal bands drawn inside the head.
  • bottomWhat sits under the head: a neck, stand, stripes or wheels.
  • mouthDrawn only on some faces, and only without banding.

Color palette

Twelve hues, 30° apart around the OKLCH color wheel, each in three tones. Tones share lightness and chroma across all hues, so every hue looks equally bright; yellows get a small lightness lift. An avatar picks three hues (primary, secondary, tertiary) that sit at least 60° apart.

Color slots

Each part takes its fill from one slot in AvatarColors. A slot draws from one of the three picked hues, and where two sources are listed the generator picks between them. Slots are what a designer recolors: changing a slot's source changes every avatar that draws that part.

Head

Nine outlines, picked with equal odds. Every other part positions itself against the head: sides hug its width, the top clears its top edge, and the face fits inside it.

Top

Picked by weight. "cap" is drawn inside the head's top edge rather than above it. "bug-eyes" changes which faces are likely, favouring "blank".

Bug eyes come in an even pair or, in about a third of bug-eyed avatars, a mismatched big-and-small pair.

Face

Picked by weight; "eyes" is by far the most common. Visor, happy and shades draw an ink panel with colors.glow details. If a face doesn't fit, the generator removes banding first, then the top.

Eye arrangements

When face is "eyes", one of eleven layouts is picked with equal odds. Pupil direction is random per avatar, so the same layout looks around differently from robot to robot.

Eye patch

About 15% of avatars with a two-eyed layout (side-eye, wonky, mismatched, uneven, googly, tiny) get an ink patch over one eye, if a strap fits.

Sides

Picked with equal odds, in colors.side. "wings" is left out for the box head, whose straight sides leave it nowhere to attach.

Banding

Picked by weight, drawn inside the head. Any banding rules out a mouth, since the bands occupy the lower face.

Bottom

Picked from a pool where "neck" appears twice, so it's the most common. Necks and stand posts use colors.neck, which falls back to AVATAR_STICK gray half the time.

Mouth

Picked with equal odds, but drawn only when the face is eyes, dots or blank, there's no banding, and the head has room. That's why "none" dominates.

Combination rules

The generator isn't a free mix. These rules decide which combinations can appear, and a style change should keep them true.

  • Three hues, far apart. Primary, secondary and tertiary hues are always at least 2 steps (60°) apart, so no two parts clash in near-identical colors.
  • Bug eyes change the face. Under top: "bug-eyes" the face is usually "blank", "dots" or "eyes", never visor or shades.
  • Mouths need space. A mouth appears only with eyes, dots or blank faces, with no banding, and on heads tall enough below the eyes.
  • Faces come first. If the chosen face doesn't fit inside the head, banding is removed; if it still doesn't fit, the top is removed too.
  • No wings on boxes. head: "box" never gets sides: "wings".
  • Patches are rare. Only two-eyed layouts get an eye patch, about 15% of the time, and only where a strap can be drawn.
  • Gaps separate parts. Every part that touches another is separated by a 3.2-unit cut, so the silhouette stays readable at 16px.

States

The avatar reflects the agent's state. Asleep, eyes close and bright details go dark; a stopped agent is also shown in grayscale. Each face type closes its eyes in its own way.

Sleeping mouths: "smile" and "grin" become "line" while asleep; the other mouths stay as they are. Bug eyes turn into short dashes.

Changing a weight, pool or palette changes which robot every existing agent gets, because the name-to-robot mapping runs through the same random sequence. Purely visual edits (colors, stroke widths, outlines) keep each agent's parts and only restyle them.
